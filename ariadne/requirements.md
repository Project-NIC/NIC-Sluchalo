# Ariadne — the requirements

*Each range is set at a realistic, reasonable maximum — what tools of this class reach, not more;
the figures are checked against them in the principle.*

What the tool must do, in ranges and orders of magnitude. No exact values and no parts — a figure
here is a bound for every page after it, and this page is its one home. What it is for is in
[`purpose.md`](purpose.md); why it changed in [`WHY.md`](WHY.md).

## Where it works

| place | range |
|---|---|
| plastered brick and drywall | cables in grooves a few centimetres under the plaster, or anywhere in a cavity |
| floors and ceilings | a cable through a slab or a screed, to about 30 cm, by a tracer's signal |
| concrete with rebar | as far as it goes — nothing promised |
| an open distribution board | the wires and terminals in front of the user, at arm's length |
| a cable in the ground | to about a metre, where a tracer drives a current along it |
| a damp wall | it must still work; how much worse is to be measured |

## The thread

| | requirement |
|---|---|
| signals | the mains at 50 Hz and any cable tracer's signal from 50 Hz to about 130 kHz, several at once, each told by its frequency |
| read by | the cable's charge and its current, so that a cable shorted further on is followed too |
| where | over the cable to about a centimetre under plaster, to about a tenth of the depth deeper; its direction to a few degrees |
| how deep | from one place, by the field in three axes, to about 10 % |
| two cables | told apart a hand's width apart |
| a building where everything lights up | the cable carrying the most of the signal's current told from the rest |

## Metal

| | requirement |
|---|---|
| what | iron and non-iron told apart; a nail, a pipe, a cable's copper, a reinforcing bar |
| how deep | with a head of about 20 cm: a nail or a coin to about 20 cm, a pipe or a bar to 30–40 cm, a large iron object to about half a metre |
| its field | its own, frequency chosen from some kilohertz up to the megahertz |

## Voltage

A sound, and a bar that grows, where the mains voltage is, a few centimetres through a dry wall;
not where to the centimetre. It never says that a cable is dead.

## Heat

| | requirement |
|---|---|
| the pictures | **Gradusnik**: an ordinary one and a thermal one, laid over each other, the mix set by the user; the ordinary one sharper, a few hundred points a side |
| the thermal one | about 256 × 192 points, up to some 25 pictures a second; a difference of about 0.05 K seen |
| what must show | a loaded cable under a few centimetres of plaster, a breaker or a loose terminal warming in a board |

## The tool in the hand

| | requirement |
|---|---|
| held | in one hand, like a metal detector; one finder at a time, chosen by the user |
| display | the Báze's; no phone, no computer needed to work |
| size and weight | a head of about 20 cm; about a kilogram in all |
| running time | a working day, some eight hours of use, on one charge of the Báze's cell |
| where it works | a building site: dust, a cold shell and a hot attic, dry and damp walls; not rain |
| storing | every picture and reading kept with its raw signal, and taken out to a computer |
| openness | every setting reachable; the raw signal, the software and the hardware design open |

## Cost

The parts of one tool: under about ten thousand Czech crowns — the thermal camera the largest share.

## Safety

The tool may show that a cable is live; it never says that one is not. It replaces neither a
voltage tester nor safe working practice.
